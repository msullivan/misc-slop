Getting a reading

  1. Lie on your back on a firm surface — a bed works, a couch with springy cushions is worse.
  2. Put the phone screen-up, flat on the lower half of your breastbone. Slightly left of centre is often stronger. Bare skin or a thin shirt; no case if it rocks.
  3. Rest a hand lightly on top to stop it sliding, or don't touch it at all. Don't talk.
  4. Wait about 10 seconds for the filters to settle and the estimate to lock. Breathing slowly through the nose helps; holding your breath for 10–15 s gives the cleanest trace.

How it works

  • Raw acceleration (including gravity) arrives through devicemotion, usually at about 60 Hz. The sample rate is measured from timestamps, and filters are designed for it.
  • A 4th-order Butterworth band-pass (5 Hz to 25 Hz, or 0.42 × the sample rate if that's lower) removes gravity, breathing and posture drift, leaving the vibration of valve closure and ejection (the SCG's AO and AC waves).
  • The squared signal is smoothed into an envelope that merges each beat's two vibrations into one bump. Heart rate comes from that envelope's autocorrelation over the last 8 s, scored as r(P) + ½·r(2P) to avoid locking on half the true rate. The median of recent estimates is shown.
  • Individual beats are local maxima of the envelope above an adaptive threshold, with a refractory window of 0.6 × the locked period. Intervals that disagree with the lock by more than 30% are discarded.
  • Readings are held when the motion level jumps (you moved) or the phone tilts more than 35°.
